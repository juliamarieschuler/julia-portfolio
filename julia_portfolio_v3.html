01 / About

Build the conditions for better work.

I work where people, structures and technology meet. I connect perspectives across silos, make friction visible and help teams turn shared understanding into decisions, ownership and continuous improvement.

Over the past years at roadsurfer, I have supported teams and leaders through growth, shifting domains and reorganizations. My work has ranged from coaching Product Owners and leadership to building an Agile Coaching team, strengthening collaboration and evolving the way an organization plans, learns and delivers.

For me, agility is not a ceremony or a framework to roll out. It is an organization's ability to see what is happening, focus on the right problem and adapt without losing direction.

“The goal is not more process. It’s clearer decisions, shared ownership and meaningful outcomes.”
02 / My method

I treat change as a problem to understand — not a solution to sell.

This is also how I approached this portfolio project: start with the purpose, understand the gap, shape a strategy, test the result and improve from evidence. The same logic helps me make organizational change tangible.

01

Understand the starting point

Define the target

Make the desired outcome explicit. Connect the change to strategic value, user needs and observable signals of success.

Map the gap & design the path

Identify what stands between today and the target. Prioritize the most relevant changes, stakeholders, communication and experiments.

Make a first version tangible, validate it with real people, check what works and refine based on feedback and outcomes.

04 / What’s next

Transformation in an AI-accelerated world.

AI changes how work gets done. It does not remove the need for judgment, shared understanding, quality or good decisions.

I’m interested in helping organizations evolve their workflows without losing the human capabilities that make change sustainable: clear priorities, critical thinking, collaboration and the confidence to challenge the first answer.

That means choosing the right problems, testing new ways of working, paying attention to quality and learning from outcomes — rather than assuming faster output automatically creates value.
